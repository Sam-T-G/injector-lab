# First-pass audit of the v0.4 model (2026-10-19)

Findings from reading the description in `original-prompt.md`. Reviewers: confirm or refute each one; do not repeat them as new findings.

1. **Fourth-power sensitivity.** Secondary-breakup cap D = 12 sigma/(rho_g v_rel^2) and tau = D^2/K give tau proportional to v_rel^-4 whenever the cap is active. Doubling the guessed v_rel cuts tau 16x. The "nominal" scenario (average of pessimistic and optimistic v_rel) sits on the steep part, and the search optimizes it. This explains 12.4%..100% and the 99.5% nominal.
2. **Two clocks.** Vaporization uses t = gas residence time Lc/u (rough estimate ~2 ms at the default case with Tc ~3200 K, MW ~23, not checked against CEA). A droplet leaving at ~20 m/s needs 12.5 ms to cross 250 mm unless gas drag accelerates it. Proposed fix: march droplets down the chamber with drag + evaporation while gas speed grows with the vaporized fraction (Priem & Heidmann, NASA TR R-67, 1960, style). This also replaces the three v_rel scenarios.
3. **Sizing ignores efficiency.** mdot = F/Isp_ideal and At = mdot c*/Pc use ideal values; with eta < 1 the engine undershoots thrust and Pc. Size with an assumed eta and iterate.
4. **Scan edge.** The chosen dP/Pc = 15% is the bottom of the scan and the low end of the usual chugging rule of thumb (often quoted 15-20% of Pc; rule of thumb). With eta saturated, the 1% window is wide and the tie-break (smallest dP, lowest Pc, shortest L*) picks the least-margin design. Results on a scan boundary should be flagged.
5. **c* cap may hide a mass leak.** With mass weights and averaging in fuel mass fraction, Jensen's inequality bounds the stream-tube mean by c*(global) while the spread stays inside the concave region of c*(f). A mean above 1 more likely means Gaussian tails beyond the wall are dropped (outer LOX ring loses more) or O/F itself was averaged. "Convex" is backwards: c* is concave near its peak. Test mass conservation instead of capping.
6. **Tool does not analyze the CAD part.** STL is inch-based (127 mm = 5.00 in, 25.4 = 1.00 in, ring radii 1.10 in and 0.75 in). Ratios 0.68/0.46 imply an ~82 mm (3.25 in) chamber; the tool's 51.5 mm chamber with scaled rings is a different injector. Holes match #54 (1.397 mm) and #56 (1.181 mm) number drills. Add an Analyze mode (fixed geometry in, predictions out) and drill-size snapping.
7. **Ring assignment.** At equal dP, A_ox/A_f = (O/F) sqrt(rho_f/rho_ox); STL area ratio (1.392/1.183)^2 = 1.385 implies O/F ~ 1.67 (rho_LOX 1141, rho_EtOH 789), so LOX-outer is consistent with the holes. But it places oxidizer at the wall; common practice is fuel-rich near the wall.
8. **Igniter heuristic uses the wrong regime.** Ignition is a startup transient (chamber near ambient, no hot gas, LOX possibly boiling in a warm manifold). Steady-state end-of-chamber fields do not describe it; flammability is not a log-normal centered on stoichiometric.
9. **Manifold thermal coupling.** Stacked blocks: LOX chills the ethanol side (viscosity up), and LOX density depends strongly on actual injection temperature; both shift O/F.
10. **Missing hardware context** in the brief: burn time, cooling method and chamber material, igniter type, feed system and tank pressures, test stand vs flight, supervision.
